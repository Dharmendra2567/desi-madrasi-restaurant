Where it all began

Born from a craving for home

Every South Indian who has moved away from home knows the feeling. It is a slow Sunday morning, and what you really want is a plate of soft idlis with coconut chutney that tastes exactly the way it did at home — not almost, not nearly, but exactly.

Desi Madrasi was created for that feeling. Hyderabad is home to thousands of families from Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka and Andhra, and to many more Hyderabadis who have fallen for dosa and filter coffee along the way. We wanted to give all of them a place where the food is honest, the welcome is warm and the flavours are unmistakably Madras.

“We don’t cook for customers. We cook for guests — the way our families cooked for anyone who walked through the door.”

So we kept the old ways. The wet grinder that hums every evening. Cast-iron tawas seasoned over years of use. Mustard seeds and curry leaves crackling in hot oil. And the brass filter that drips its decoction one patient drop at a time.

Inside Our Kitchen

The 13-hour journey of one idli

Soft idlis are not a recipe — they are a routine. Here is what happens before that first plate reaches your table at seven in the morning.

6 PM

Soak

Idli rice and whole urad dal are washed and soaked separately in purified water, each for exactly as long as it needs.

10 PM

Stone-grind

The granite wet-grinder turns the dal light and fluffy while ice-cold water keeps the batter from warming.

Overnight

Ferment

Seasoned with rock salt, the batter rests and rises on its own, developing that gentle, lovable tang.

7 AM

Steam & serve

Ladled into idli plates, steamed and served within minutes — with sambar, coconut and tomato chutneys.
