Where it all began

Born from a craving for home

Every South Indian who has moved away from home knows the feeling. It is a lazy Sunday afternoon, and what you really want is a plate of biryani that tastes exactly the way it did at home — not almost, not nearly, but exactly.

Desi Madrasi was created for that feeling. Hyderabad loves its bagara and dum biryani, and the city is also home to thousands of families from Tamil Nadu who miss the Madras-style biryani and sukka of home. We wanted one place for both — where the food is honest, the welcome is warm and the flavours are unmistakably Desi and Madrasi.

“We don’t cook for customers. We cook for guests — the way our families cooked for anyone who walked through the door.”

So we kept the old ways. Whole spices roasted and ground in our own kitchen. Meat marinated for hours before it meets the rice. Curry leaves and shallots crackling in hot oil for the sukka. And meals served on a fresh banana leaf.

Inside Our Kitchen

The journey of one biryani

Good biryani is not a recipe — it is a routine. Here is what happens before that first plate reaches your table.

Step 1

Marinate

Chicken and mutton are marinated in curd, ginger-garlic and our own spice blend, and left to rest for hours.

Step 2

Roast & grind

Whole spices are dry-roasted in small batches and ground fresh for the biryani and sukka masalas.

Step 3

Cook slow

Rice and meat cook together in the handi with tomato, mint and green chilli, so every grain takes on the masala.

Step 4

Rest & serve

The handi rests, then it is opened and served hot on a banana leaf — with raita and salan on the side.
